Valid but duplicate: QuillShield on bounties and contests

From March to November 2026 the QuillShield team filed 43 findings in Solidity bug bounties and audit contests. Of the 20 with a final ruling, 12 were duplicates of earlier reports (11 judged valid) and 8 were rejected. None was judged unique. Another 18, from one competition, are still in judging, and 5 have no result on record.

Published 18 November 2026. 11 minute read. By the QuillShield team.

We wanted a test of QuillShield that we could not grade ourselves, and live bug bounties and audit contests are that test. Other researchers hunt the same code at the same time, and a judge who does not work for us decides what counts. So from March to November 2026 our team ran QuillShield on the programs it entered and filed what held up under human review. This post lists every result we can, the misses included.

What we did and why

Our EVMbench run measures how many known bugs QuillShield finds, and we graded it ourselves. In a bounty or a contest nobody knows the bugs in advance, the platform or the protocol's team rules on each report, and a finding only counts in full if it is real, in scope and not already reported.

Between March and November 2026 the QuillShield team filed 43 findings in 20 Solidity programs, 7 audit contests and 13 bug bounties. QuillShield also ran on at least five more Solidity programs where we filed nothing.

How each run worked

The team's practice was to run a full QuillShield audit of the in-scope code before anyone reviewed it by hand. That was already how it worked in March, when QuillShield's report on the Intuition contest came back with proofs of concept. In July the team made it a rule, with manual review limited to two or three contracts out of ten. The same month it started skipping projects that top audit firms had already reviewed heavily.

Auditors then triaged. They checked each QuillShield finding against the program's known issues and earlier audits, and sorted it into real, false, already known, or needs a proof of concept. The team's written process says nothing goes out until a person has reproduced it with a failing test or a proof of concept. The team also used other AI workflows and skills, fuzzing and manual review, and later a tool that maps a protocol's external dependencies. So every result below is the team's, with QuillShield doing the opening pass.

One staking protocol's bug bounty shows the effort. QuillShield reported eight findings and the team filed one. The engagement log records 25 hours, split into 10 to understand the protocol, 2 for setup, 3 to triage QuillShield's output, 3 of manual review, 4 for the proof of concept and 3 for the report.

Three public contests

Intuition on Code4rena: one real Medium, found 42 times

Code4rena ran the Intuition audit from 4 to 9 March 2026, on five contracts and 844 lines of Solidity. QuillShield's report came with proofs of concept, and our auditors checked each finding. One candidate said a governance setting could change reward maths partway through an epoch. Asked for a proof of concept, QuillShield's validation argued against its own finding, because the setting belongs to a trusted role behind a three-day timelock. The auditor moved on.

What we filed was judged a Medium. Checkpoints made at an epoch boundary could qualify for the previous epoch's rewards. The published report lists it as M-02 and credits 42 wardens and teams with it, QuillAudits_Team among them. The contest had two Mediums and no Highs, and we did not report the other one. Intuition fixed both, and a mitigation review confirmed the fixes.

Metric on Sherlock: our note said design choice

Metric's contest ran on Sherlock from 6 to 27 July 2026. Our engagement log lists 77 contracts in scope, all reviewed with QuillShield and a set of audit skills. We filed one Medium on share valuation, and the team's note that day called it "valid but design choice". Nobody logged the final ruling, so it counts as no result on record. Every finding Sherlock published for this contest is a Medium.

Monetrix on Code4rena: a second swing that missed

Monetrix ran on Code4rena from 24 April to 4 May 2026. We took it on next to a bounty review already under way, betting the setup would carry over. Our one finding is not among the awarded findings in the published results, and our tracker records it as not accepted. The tracker does not say what the finding was.

What a valid duplicate means

A valid duplicate means the bug was real and someone else reported it first. It is not a win and it is not a loss. In a contest, everyone who filed the bug shares the credit. On a bug bounty, the earliest report takes it and later ones are closed as duplicates.

Eleven of our findings ended as valid duplicates, and none of the 20 with a final ruling was judged unique. Common Solidity bug patterns are widely known, and most of our findings were filed by other researchers at about the same time. On Intuition, 42 wardens and teams filed the same Medium.

So the team changed its method. It started building about 100 bug patterns for each type of protocol, a map of each protocol's external dependencies and a shared list of invariants. The dependency tool was part of our entry in the competition still in judging.

What this says about an AI review before a bounty or contest

Look at the duplicates from the protocol's side. If QuillShield and a small team found a bug that 41 others also found, that bug was there to be caught before the contest opened. A protocol paying a crowd for it is paying for something a scan before launch would have flagged.

So run an AI reviewer before you open a bounty or book a contest, fix what it finds, and let the crowd spend its time on bugs that need a person. Don't expect the AI pass to replace that step. Three more lessons came from the misses.

  • Treat severity as a starting guess. On one bounty a High we filed was judged Medium, and our July notes say QuillShield needs each platform's severity rules.
  • Learn what the protocol calls intended. Two Highs on bounties were rejected as design choices, and on Intuition QuillShield's own validation argued a candidate down for the same reason.
  • Read the program's rules before spending the hours. Three findings were closed as out of scope, and on one bounty our three Lows sat below the program's minimum severity.

The benchmark points the same way. On EVMbench, a set of known bugs from past audits, the QuillShield alpha found 98 of 117 (83.8%) in our own run, as the EVMbench post explains. QuillShield is good at the bugs many reviewers can find. That is worth a lot before launch and much less in a race to file.

Limits

  • QuillShield did the opening pass, but people triaged, reproduced and wrote up every finding, with other tools as well. Our records name QuillShield as the source for only a few findings, including the Intuition Medium and the staking bounty's Medium.
  • Twenty-three of the 43 findings have no final ruling. Eighteen are in judging, one has had no reply since May and four have no result on record.
  • Most programs can't be named under platform rules, so only the three named contests can be checked against public results.
  • The engagement log records time for one program only.
  • We count rulings, not rewards. This post makes no claim about what any finding paid.
  • These are our own records. The platforms did the judging, and we did the counting.

Common questions

Did QuillShield find a unique bug in a live bug bounty or audit contest?

Not in these records. From March to November 2026 the QuillShield team filed 43 findings in 20 Solidity bug bounties and audit contests. Of the 20 findings with a final ruling, 11 were judged valid duplicates, 1 was closed as a duplicate and 8 were rejected. None was judged unique. Another 18, all from one competition, were still in judging on 18 November 2026.

What does a valid duplicate mean in a bug bounty or audit contest?

It means the judge agreed the bug is real and someone else reported it first. It is not a win and not a loss. In the Intuition audit on Code4rena in March 2026, for example, 42 wardens and teams were credited with the same Medium, QuillAudits_Team among them.

Did QuillShield find every bug the team filed?

Our records do not say for most findings. QuillShield ran a full audit of the in-scope Solidity code before manual review. People then triaged its output, reproduced each finding and wrote it up, and they also used other AI workflows and skills, fuzzing and manual review. The records name QuillShield as the source for a few findings, including the Intuition Medium.

Why are most of the programs not named?

Bug bounty and contest platforms limit what researchers may say about reports. We name a contest only when its results and report are public, describe a bounty without naming it when the result reveals nothing exploitable, and keep everything else in the totals.
